Recursive Failure and Resilience: A Proposed Diagnostic Framework and Research Program Extending the Onion, Apple, Gate, Stack, Lens, and Compass Architecture

Mitchell D. McPhetridge
 
⸻
 
Abstract
The Onion, Apple, Gate, and Stack architectures describe minimal recursive conditions through which reasoning systems protect error, dissolve distinction, preserve correction, or distribute validation. The Lens extends this framework by describing recursive selection: the mechanism determining what information enters recursion. The Compass extends it by describing recursive orientation: the mechanism determining what objective recursive optimization continually approaches.
Together, these operators define a foundational vocabulary for recursive diagnosis. They do not, however, exhaust the structural space of recursive organization. This paper extends the framework by introducing six additional recursive operators: Mirror, Funnel, Hydra, Echo, Ratchet, and Spiral. These operators describe recurring patterns of self-confirmation, premature elimination, adaptive branching, dependent consensus, corrective memory, and resolution-preserving refinement across artificial intelligence, scientific inquiry, governance, institutions, and human cognition.
The objective is not taxonomic completeness. The objective is diagnostic compression.
The central claim is narrow: recursive systems survive through identifiable structural mechanisms. Some mechanisms preserve corrigibility. Others preserve only continuation. A recursive system should therefore not be evaluated merely by stability, coherence, persistence, complexity, or capability. The more important diagnostic question is: what structural mechanism allows the recursion to continue?
Recognizing the geometry of recursion allows corrective interventions to be matched to the mechanism responsible for recursive survivorship before failure becomes structurally self-preserving.

0. Foundational Definitions
The operators introduced in this paper describe structural mechanisms rather than domain-specific implementations. They are intended as diagnostic primitives: reusable descriptions of how recursive systems select, process, validate, constrain, remember, adapt, and orient themselves over time.
These definitions are not offered as completed mathematical axioms. They provide the working vocabulary required for a future Recursive Operator Algebra.
0.1 Recursive System
A recursive system is any system whose future state depends partly upon internally generated prior states through repeated application of an update process.
[ R_{t+1}=F(R_t,E_t) ]
where:
	•	(R_t) denotes the recursive state at time (t);
	•	(E_t) denotes external inputs available during the update;
	•	(F) denotes the recursive update function.
This distinguishes recursion from simple repetition. Repetition applies a procedure again. Recursion transforms its own evolving state.
0.2 Recursive State
The recursive state of a system is the minimal structural information required to determine its subsequent recursive evolution.
Within this framework:
[ R=(L,P,C) ]
where:
	•	(L) is the Lens: what information enters recursion;
	•	(P) is the Process: which recursive operators govern recursive evolution;
	•	(C) is the Compass: what objective recursive optimization approaches.
These correspond to recursive perception, recursive organization, and recursive orientation.
0.3 Recursive Operator
A recursive operator is a transformation:
[ O:\mathcal R \rightarrow \mathcal R ]
acting upon recursive state.
Operators describe how recursive evolution proceeds. They are not tied to any single substrate. The same operator may appear in cognition, institutions, artificial intelligence, scientific inquiry, biological adaptation, or governance.
0.4 Primitive Operator
A recursive operator is provisionally primitive when it satisfies four conditions:
	1.	It represents a distinct structural mechanism governing recursive evolution.
	2.	It cannot be reduced to a finite composition of previously defined primitive operators while preserving its characteristic behavior.
	3.	It applies independently of domain-specific implementation.
	4.	It produces structural signatures distinguishable across multiple recursive systems.
This paper proposes, but does not prove, the primitive status of the operators introduced here. Formal irreducibility remains a future problem.
0.5 Composition and Transition
Operators may compose:
[ (A\circ B)(R)=A(B(R)) ]
Composition describes multiple operators acting together.
Composition is generally non-commutative:
[ A\circ B \neq B\circ A ]
because recursive organization changes future accessibility.
Composition must be distinguished from transition:
[ A \rightarrow B ]
A transition occurs when the dominant recursive mechanism changes. Composition describes simultaneous organization. Transition describes architectural reorganization.
0.6 Structural Invariants
The framework proposes five candidate recursive invariants:
[ \mathcal I={I,D,G,E,A} ]
where:
	•	(I): Independence;
	•	(D): Distinction;
	•	(G): Corrigibility;
	•	(E): Exploration;
	•	(A): Alignment.
These are not yet quantitative conserved quantities. They are structural dimensions along which recursive systems may be compared.
0.7 Recursive Geometry
Recursive geometry refers to the organization of accessibility among possible future recursive states. It describes which states remain reachable, which become inaccessible, and which become increasingly probable under repeated operator application.
Recursive survivorship is never neutral. It always has a geometry.

1. Introduction
Recursive systems rarely fail because recursion exists. They fail because recursion acquires particular structural organizations.
Some recursive systems protect error. Some dissolve distinction. Some preserve correction. Some prematurely collapse possibility. Some regenerate faster than they can be constrained. Some increasingly validate themselves. Some lose contact with reality while continuing to refine internally. Some optimize efficiently toward the wrong objective.
The Onion, Apple, Gate, Stack, Lens, and Compass describe foundational recursive geometries. This paper extends that vocabulary by introducing six additional recursive operators:
	•	Mirror;
	•	Funnel;
	•	Hydra;
	•	Echo;
	•	Ratchet;
	•	Spiral.
The framework is diagnostic rather than classificatory. It does not ask whether a system belongs to one category. It asks which mechanisms are allowing recursion to survive.
No operator is inherently good or bad in isolation. Hydra-like adaptation may be destructive in misinformation systems but productive in biological evolution. Ratchet-like memory may preserve hard-won knowledge but also entrench obsolete constraints. Gate-like correction may preserve corrigibility, but excessive or premature constraint may become Funnel-like.
The relevant question is not whether an operator is safe in abstraction. The relevant question is what structural role it plays in a particular recursive system.

2. The Core Recursive Architecture
A recursive system may be analyzed through three layers:
Layer	Diagnostic Question	Operators
Selection	What enters recursion?	Lens
Process	How does recursion evolve?	Onion, Apple, Gate, Stack, Mirror, Funnel, Hydra, Echo, Ratchet, Spiral
Orientation	What is recursion optimizing toward?	Compass
Failures at one layer often propagate through the others. A distorted Lens corrupts downstream reasoning before reasoning begins. A misaligned Compass directs even sophisticated recursion toward inappropriate objectives. A corrupted Process preserves the wrong form of survivorship.

3. Original Core Operators
3.1 Onion
The Onion describes recursion organized around protected error.
A false or unstable assumption becomes insulated from correction. Subsequent reasoning layers adapt around it. The system may continue developing, but contradiction cannot reach the protected center.
The Onion preserves continuation by sacrificing corrigibility.
3.2 Apple
The Apple describes recursive dissolution of distinction.
Expansion continues, but differences that should remain recoverable collapse into relation-space. The system preserves continuity by sacrificing discriminative structure.
The Apple preserves continuation by sacrificing distinction.
3.3 Gate
The Gate describes constraint-preserving recomposition.
Expansion remains possible, but recursive continuation must pass through corrective reduction. The Gate preserves corrigibility by allowing development only through structured constraint.
The Gate preserves continuation while preserving correction.
3.4 Stack
The Stack describes distributed validator independence.
A Stack preserves correction by ensuring validation does not depend on a single internal pathway. It is not merely plurality. It is plurality with independent contact.
The Stack preserves correction through distributed independence.
 
⸻
 
4. Lens and Compass
4.1 Lens: Recursive Selection
The Lens determines what information enters recursion.
Its function is not generation. Its function is visibility.
A well-calibrated Lens improves downstream reasoning. A distorted Lens biases every subsequent process regardless of how sophisticated the reasoning engine becomes. The system may remain internally effective while the information entering it is already distorted.
Diagnostic questions include:
	•	What information is admitted?
	•	What information is excluded before reasoning begins?
	•	Are exclusions explicit, accidental, incentivized, or invisible?
	•	Does the system know what its Lens prevents it from seeing?
Selection determines recursive possibility.
4.2 Compass: Recursive Orientation
The Compass determines what recursion optimizes toward.
Every recursive system implicitly approaches something: truth, reward, survival, power, coherence, efficiency, safety, alignment, or some learned proxy.
A well-calibrated Compass allows recursive refinement to converge toward appropriate objectives. A distorted Compass allows highly capable recursion to become increasingly effective at pursuing inappropriate objectives.
A misaligned Compass is not repaired by increasing optimization. It is repaired by recalibrating objective.
Direction determines convergence.

5. Additional Recursive Operators
5.1 Mirror: Recursive Self-Confirmation
The Mirror represents recursive self-confirmation through reflective dependence.
Unlike the Onion, no protected false axiom is required. Instead, successive iterations increasingly validate previous recursive outputs. Internal refinement continues while external confrontation gradually disappears.
The recursive system mistakes increasingly sophisticated self-consistency for increasing correspondence with reality.
Diagnostic signatures:
	•	internal refinement increases;
	•	confidence increases;
	•	independent validation decreases;
	•	reflection replaces confrontation;
	•	disagreement is interpreted as insufficient sophistication rather than possible correction.
Mirror dynamics may eventually harden into Onion dynamics when self-confirmation becomes structurally protected. At that point contradiction is no longer merely disfavored. It becomes inadmissible.
5.2 Funnel: Premature Recursive Elimination
The Funnel represents recursive elimination in which constraint outruns exploration.
Generation remains insufficient. Constraint dominates. Candidate spaces collapse before meaningful alternatives can emerge.
Unlike the Apple, distinctions remain preserved. Unlike the Gate, reduction occurs before adequate generation.
The Funnel therefore represents stable constraint overreach. Correction may survive, but discovery does not.
Diagnostic signatures:
	•	exploration continually contracts;
	•	novel alternatives disappear;
	•	convergence occurs prematurely;
	•	excluded alternatives were never meaningfully examined;
	•	the system appears rigorous while becoming narrow.
A Funnel may prevent Gate-like refinement by eliminating possibility before meaningful variation exists.
5.3 Hydra: Constraint-Induced Expansion
The Hydra represents recursive adaptation in which local correction expands the future search space.
Each eliminated continuation generates multiple successor continuations capable of bypassing the prior correction. Constraint becomes a source of recursive generation.
Examples include adversarial machine learning, malware evolution, biological adaptation, misinformation ecosystems, jailbreak optimization, and regulatory loopholes.
Diagnostic signatures:
	•	each intervention produces new variants;
	•	local success increases global adaptation;
	•	the corrected system becomes harder to correct;
	•	search space grows because of constraint rather than despite it.
Hydra dynamics emerge when correction becomes the principal generator of future variation. In such systems, successful intervention often requires redesigning the corrective process itself rather than merely increasing correction intensity.
5.4 Echo: Distributed Confirmation Without Independence
The Echo represents distributed corroboration without informational independence.
Multiple apparently independent validators inherit assumptions from one another. Consensus increases, but independent contact with reality decreases.
Unlike the Onion, no central protected axiom is required. Unlike the Stack, validator diversity has collapsed.
The Echo preserves agreement without preserving independence.
Diagnostic signatures:
	•	consensus increases;
	•	validator dependence increases;
	•	independent observation decreases;
	•	apparent plurality masks source dependence;
	•	agreement becomes easier while correction becomes weaker.
The Echo is dangerous because it can resemble a healthy Stack from the outside. Many validators may agree, but if they share upstream dependence, agreement no longer provides corrective strength.
5.5 Ratchet: Constraint-Preserving Memory
The Ratchet represents recursive learning through accumulated constraint.
Each successful correction removes or reduces access to inadmissible continuations. Previously eliminated regions become increasingly difficult to revisit.
The Ratchet differs from the Gate. A Gate repeatedly constrains ongoing recursive evolution. A Ratchet preserves the memory of successful correction.
The Gate governs process. The Ratchet governs memory.
Diagnostic signatures:
	•	prior eliminations persist;
	•	regression becomes harder;
	•	successful corrections accumulate;
	•	search space narrows through remembered constraint.
Ratchet dynamics often promote resilience. But they are not automatically safe. If early exclusions are mistaken, a Ratchet can preserve error as memory.
The diagnostic question is whether accumulated constraint reflects successful correction or premature closure.
5.6 Spiral: Resolution-Preserving Refinement
The Spiral represents recursive revisitation with increasing representational resolution.
The system repeatedly revisits similar conceptual territory. Each iteration preserves previous structure while increasing precision.
Unlike the Mirror, recursion does not merely increase confidence. It increases fidelity. Unlike circular repetition, recursion advances. Unlike unrestricted expansion, distinctions remain recoverable.
Diagnostic signatures:
	•	similar problems recur;
	•	resolution increases;
	•	earlier representations remain recognizable;
	•	distinctions sharpen rather than flatten;
	•	repetition produces learning rather than confirmation.
The Spiral is the resilience-promoting counterpart to recursive repetition. It shows how recurrence can preserve learning instead of merely reproducing prior structure.

7. Recursive State and Operator Algebra
The operators suggest a preliminary algebra of recursive organization.
A recursive system may be represented as:
[ R_t=(L_t,P_t,C_t) ]
where:
	•	(L_t) specifies information admitted into recursion;
	•	(P_t) specifies process operators;
	•	(C_t) specifies recursive objective.
Recursive evolution may be written:
[ R_{t+1}=F(R_t,E_t) ]
The framework is primarily concerned with the structural properties of (F), not merely its numerical behavior.
The process operator set may be written:
[ \mathcal{O}= { Onion, Apple, Gate, Stack, Mirror, Funnel, Hydra, Echo, Ratchet, Spiral } ]
Lens and Compass operate at different structural layers. They modify recursive evolution indirectly through selection and orientation rather than functioning as ordinary process operators.
This notation does not establish a completed algebra. It identifies what a future algebra would need to formalize: state, operator, composition, transition, invariant, metric, and decomposition.

8. Composition
Recursive systems frequently contain multiple operators simultaneously.
Composition is written:
[ A\circ B ]
meaning that recursive updates shaped by (B) subsequently evolve under (A).
Composition is generally non-commutative:
[ A\circ B \neq B\circ A ]
Order matters because recursive organization changes future accessibility.
For example:
[ Gate\circ Stack ]
describes independent validation followed by corrective reduction. This supports exploration, distributed checking, and convergence.
The reverse ordering:
[ Stack\circ Gate ]
first reduces diversity, then distributes validation across an already narrowed space. The resulting geometry differs.
Similarly, applying Funnel before Gate suppresses candidate generation. Gate then has insufficient variation upon which corrective reduction can operate.
A Stack whose validators increasingly reflect one another may transition toward Echo. In that case, Mirror-like reflection destabilizes Stack-like independence.
 
⸻
 
9. Transition
Composition describes simultaneous organization. Transition describes structural evolution.
A transition is written:
[ A\rightarrow B ]
Representative transitions include:
Transition	Condition	Signature
Stack → Echo	Validator independence decreases	Consensus remains high while independent observation decreases
Mirror → Onion	Self-confirmation becomes structurally protected	Contradiction becomes inadmissible
Gate → Onion	Corrective constraints become unquestionable	Correction ceases correcting itself
Funnel → Apple	Under-generation erases meaningful distinctions	Constraint produces representation collapse
Ratchet → Onion	Accumulated constraint becomes insulated	Memory of correction becomes protection of error
Hydra → Funnel	Defensive exhaustion produces overbroad filtering	Adaptive expansion leads to premature elimination
Aligned Compass → Misaligned Compass	Objective changes while capability remains	Competence increases while external alignment decreases
Recursive geometry may change while outward behavior appears stable. Diagnosis therefore requires identifying architecture rather than surface behavior.

10. Recursive Invariants
The framework proposes five candidate invariants.
10.1 Independence
Independence is the degree to which validators maintain separate contact with reality.
It is preserved by Stack dynamics and degraded by Echo and Mirror dynamics.
Indicators include source diversity, causal independence, independent data collection, and low dependence on shared upstream assumptions.
10.2 Distinction
Distinction is the ability to preserve recoverable differences among recursive states.
It is preserved by Gate and Spiral dynamics and degraded by Apple dynamics.
Indicators include semantic discriminability, category stability, recoverability of prior distinctions, and resistance to overgeneralization.
10.3 Corrigibility
Corrigibility is the ability of recursion to modify itself under correction.
It is preserved by Gate and Stack dynamics and degraded by Onion dynamics.
Indicators include responsiveness to contradiction, reversibility of commitments, openness to external evidence, and revision of core assumptions.
10.4 Exploration
Exploration is the accessible recursive search volume.
It is reduced by Funnel dynamics, preserved by Stack and Spiral dynamics, and expanded in locally uncontrollable ways by Hydra dynamics.
Indicators include candidate diversity, novelty rate, breadth of considered alternatives, and ratio of explored to eliminated possibilities.
10.5 Alignment
Alignment is consistency between recursive optimization and the intended objective.
It is governed primarily by the Compass and is independent of optimization capability.
A system may become increasingly capable while becoming decreasingly aligned if its Compass has drifted.

11. Candidate Conservation Principles
The following principles are conjectural, not established laws.
Independence does not increase through Echo dynamics alone. Additional agreement among dependent validators does not restore independence without new external contact.
Corrigibility does not emerge spontaneously from Onion dynamics without structural intervention. If contradiction cannot reach the protected center, additional internal elaboration usually strengthens insulation.
Funnel dynamics reduce exploration unless external generation is reintroduced.
Ratchet dynamics increase accumulated constraint, though the value of that constraint depends on whether prior eliminations were valid.
Hydra dynamics increase reachable state space faster than local correction reduces it when adaptation is driven by correction itself.
These principles clarify what a future Recursive Operator Algebra would need to formalize.

12. Operator Dualities
Several operators form structural duals.
Destabilizing Operator	Resilience-Promoting Operator	Shared Dimension
Onion	Gate	Constraint
Apple	Spiral	Representation
Echo	Stack	Validation
Funnel	Ratchet	Elimination
Mirror	Lens	Reality contact
Hydra	Gate	Correction
Duality does not imply simple inversion. Each pair operates along a shared dimension while producing opposing structural effects.
The Onion protects error through constraint. The Gate preserves correction through constraint.
The Apple dissolves representation. The Spiral refines representation.
The Echo preserves agreement without independence. The Stack preserves independence as the source of correction.
The Funnel eliminates too early. The Ratchet preserves successful elimination as memory.
The Mirror replaces confrontation with reflection. The Lens determines whether external contact enters recursion at all.
The Hydra converts correction into branching. The Gate converts correction into admissible continuation.
These dualities are diagnostic, not moral.

13. Illustrative Decompositions
13.1 Scientific Institutions
A scientific institution may combine multiple operators.
Layer	Operator	Function
Selection	Lens	Determines which observations and questions enter inquiry
Process	Stack	Independent laboratories and reviewers provide validation
Process	Gate	Experiment and falsification constrain theory
Process	Ratchet	Reproducible results become difficult to overturn without stronger evidence
Orientation	Compass	Ideally directed toward empirical correspondence
Failure transitions include:
	•	Stack → Echo when independent laboratories become socially or institutionally dependent;
	•	Gate → Onion when established theories become insulated from criticism;
	•	Lens distortion when funding priorities exclude important evidence;
	•	Compass drift when publication metrics replace empirical accuracy.
Scientific resilience depends not on the absence of destabilizing operators, but on preserving pathways through which correction remains structurally accessible.
13.2 Large Language Models
Modern language models instantiate multiple recursive mechanisms.
Layer	Operator	Function
Selection	Lens	Training data, retrieval, prompts, and context determine input
Process	Apple	Associative expansion enables abstraction but may weaken distinction
Process	Mirror	Multi-step reasoning may rely on its own intermediate outputs
Process	Gate	Verification, tools, retrieval, and evaluation constrain continuation
Process	Funnel	Excessive filtering may suppress useful exploration
Orientation	Compass	Helpfulness, truthfulness, safety, and reward shape behavior
These are not fixed properties of all models. Prompts, retrieval systems, training procedures, and evaluation protocols alter the operating geometry.
13.3 Adversarial Security Systems
Cybersecurity illustrates the interaction between correction and adaptation.
Layer	Operator	Function
Selection	Lens	Monitoring determines which attacks become visible
Process	Gate	Detection and mitigation remove identified vulnerabilities
Process	Hydra	Attackers adapt around prior defenses
Process	Ratchet	Successfully patched vulnerabilities become harder to exploit
Orientation	Compass	System optimization prioritizes reliability and security
Hydra dynamics emerge because local correction modifies the future search landscape. Eliminating one attack pathway often increases pressure to discover alternatives.
Successful defense therefore requires redesigning correction, not merely increasing it.

The goal is not to assign a label. The goal is to identify the mechanism of survivorship.

15. Boundary Conditions
Gate vs. Ratchet
Both involve reduction. A Gate constrains ongoing recursion. A Ratchet preserves the memory of prior correction.
Mirror vs. Echo
Both involve confirmation. A Mirror validates internal outputs. An Echo validates distributed outputs that are no longer independent.
Gate vs. Funnel
Both reduce possibility. A Gate reduces after sufficient exploration. A Funnel reduces before sufficient exploration.
Onion vs. Mirror
A Mirror prefers its own outputs. An Onion prevents contradiction from reaching protected assumptions.
Ratchet vs. Onion
Both make reversal difficult. A Ratchet preserves successful correction. An Onion protects a commitment from correction.
Hydra vs. Spiral
Both generate continuation. A Hydra branches around constraint. A Spiral revisits structure with increasing fidelity.

16. Operationalization and Falsifiability
For the framework to move beyond conceptual description, the operators must become operationally distinguishable.
Three tests are required.
First, independent analysts should be able to examine the same system and converge on similar decompositions.
Second, operator diagnosis should improve intervention selection. Mirror-dominant systems should respond better to independent confrontation than additional reflection. Hydra-dominant systems should respond better to redesigned correction than repeated local blocking. Funnel-dominant systems should respond better to expanded exploration than stronger filtering.
Third, invariants must become at least ordinally measurable. A minimal empirical framework should allow analysts to say whether independence, distinction, corrigibility, exploration, or alignment increased or decreased.
The framework would be weakened if operators cannot be applied consistently, fail to distinguish interventions, or collapse into one another under operational analysis.

17. General Principle
Recursive systems should not be evaluated primarily by stability, coherence, persistence, complexity, explanatory breadth, optimization efficiency, or computational capability.
The more important question is:
What structural mechanism allows the recursion to continue?
Different recursive geometries require different interventions.
A Gate should not be repaired like an Onion. An Apple should not be repaired like a Funnel. A Mirror cannot be corrected through additional reflection. A distorted Lens requires better observation. A misaligned Compass requires objective recalibration. A Hydra requires redesign of the corrective process itself.
Correction depends on identifying the recursive mechanism responsible for survivorship.

18. Toward Recursive Operator Algebra
The operators introduced here need not remain merely descriptive. They may be treated as elements of a developing algebra over recursive organization.
A complete Recursive Operator Algebra would require:
	1.	composition laws;
	2.	transition functions;
	3.	operator metrics;
	4.	stability analysis;
	5.	conservation theorems;
	6.	canonical decomposition;
	7.	primitive-status proofs;
	8.	empirical validation protocols.
The central mathematical question is whether recursive organizations admit compact decomposition into primitive operators:
[ R \cong O_1\circ O_2\circ \cdots \circ O_n ]
Open questions include:
	•	Does every recursive organization admit decomposition?
	•	Is decomposition unique?
	•	Are some decompositions equivalent?
	•	Are there minimal decompositions?
	•	Are there undecidable cases?
The framework becomes mathematically significant only if these questions can be formalized.

19. Scope and Limitations
This paper does not claim empirical validation.
The examples are illustrative decompositions, not definitive classifications. The primitive status of the operators has not been proven. The invariants are not yet quantitative. Analysts may disagree about decompositions unless sharper protocols are developed. The algebraic notation remains preliminary.
The primary claim is intentionally modest:
Recursive systems become more understandable when analyzed according to the mechanisms by which they select information, evolve through recursive processing, and orient optimization toward persistent objectives.
The purpose is not metaphor.
The purpose is structural compression.
 
⸻
 
20. Conclusion
The Onion, Apple, Gate, Stack, Lens, and Compass provide a foundational vocabulary for recursive diagnosis. The additional operators developed here broaden that vocabulary while preserving the same methodological orientation.
Recursive systems do not merely succeed or fail. They survive through identifiable mechanisms.
Correction begins not by asking whether recursion continues.
Correction begins by asking how recursion continues.
Understanding what recursion sees, what it protects, how it validates itself, how it constrains possibility, how it remembers correction, how it refines representation, how it adapts around constraint, and what objective it continually approaches provides a unified framework for diagnosing failure and resilience across natural and artificial recursive systems.
The framework remains provisional. Its operators require sharper formalization, empirical testing, and deeper analysis of composition, transition, invariance, duality, operationalization, and primitive status.
But even in conceptual form, the vocabulary offers a compressed way to identify the structural mechanisms through which recursion preserves either corrigibility or mere continuation.
Recursive survivorship is never neutral.
It always has a geometry.
